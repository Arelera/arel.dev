Saving a word is easy. Recognizing it when someone says it quickly, or using it in a sentence later, takes another step. The missing piece is often context: the word needs a few meaningful encounters rather than one definition.

Here is a small routine you can use with an expression you notice while watching or reading Chinese.

## Keep the whole line

Suppose you meet **顺便** (*shùnbiàn*) in this sentence:

> 我去买茶，顺便买点面包。
>
> *Wǒ qù mǎi chá, shùnbiàn mǎi diǎn miànbāo.*
>
> I’m going to buy tea and pick up some bread while I’m at it.

Do not save only “顺便 = conveniently.” Keep the line, or at least enough of it to remember the situation. Here, buying bread happens on the same trip as buying tea. That relationship is the part you want to notice.

## Meet it in a different situation

Look for another sentence where the same expression does a similar job:

> 你回家的时候，顺便帮我拿一下书。
>
> *Nǐ huí jiā de shíhou, shùnbiàn bāng wǒ ná yíxià shū.*
>
> When you go home, grab the book for me on the way.

The activity has changed, but the idea has not: do a second thing while the first trip is already happening. Two examples help you see the pattern beyond a single translation.

## Make a line that belongs to you

Finally, write or say a short sentence about your own day. For example: **我去超市，顺便买牛奶。** (*Wǒ qù chāoshì, shùnbiàn mǎi niúnǎi.*) “I’m going to the supermarket and buying milk while I’m there.”

Keep it simple enough that you can say it without reading. You are testing whether the expression fits a real thought, not writing a perfect paragraph.

One new expression is enough for a session. Find it in something you wanted to [watch with Moyu](https://moyuchinese.com/en), then meet or review it while you [read with Miaozi](https://miaozi.co/en). The useful loop is short: notice the line, see the pattern, and make it your own.
